How writing together works

Here is what happens when two of you open the same article.

One person has the text

That person types. Everyone else sees the words appear as they are written, and can take the text over with one tap. Whoever takes it over is asked first, and the person who had it is told what happened. Nothing gets lost, and they can take it straight back.

Nobody has to wait for the gallery

The gallery and the article settings are never locked. You can all drag tiles, set the date and change the address at the same time, and you see each other doing it. The order you leave the tiles in is the order readers see.

You can go back

Press Save version and Texttile keeps the title and text as they are. Later you can see what changed word by word, and put an old version back in the editor. The log lists every edit, handover, tile move and upload.

Why we
made it

We spent three weeks in Polynesia and kept one journal between us. We took turns writing, and whoever was not writing fixed things afterwards. The photos were the harder part. Not picking them, but getting a thousand pictures into a gallery in an order that made sense, together.

So I built imaedge for that part, and we tried it on the next holiday, in Mexico. Uploading held up. Tiles arrive in the order their EXIF timestamps give them. Drag one and Texttile rewrites its timestamp to match, so the order stays valid whoever opens the folder later.

What was still missing was the text around it. The family blog it should have gone on ran on WordPress. That meant updates every few weeks, a plugin the author had abandoned, and bots at the login page around the clock. So after Mexico, with the gallery proven, I wrote a minimal CMS around it. It has to work for the two of us on the next holiday first, but it is ready for other people. The longer version is on the blog it was made for.

What it does, and what it leaves out.

It does this

  • Everyone writes in the same article.One person has the text and types. The others read along as it happens, and can take it over with one tap.
  • You sort the gallery together.Drag the tiles around, from as many phones as there are people. Each article has one gallery, and the order you leave it in is the order readers see.
  • Markdown, saved while you type.Paste or drop a photo into the text and it gets uploaded and inserted, the way GitHub does it.
  • Versions.Save version keeps the title and text as they are. You can see what changed word by word and put an old one back.
  • A log for every article.Every edit, handover, tile move and upload, newest first.
  • Articles and pages.Tags get their own archive page, pages appear in the site menu on their own, and a date in the future schedules the article.
  • Comments appear straight away.You can ask readers to confirm their address first. The spam filter is a honeypot, a timer and a rate limit.
  • An email when you publish.Readers subscribe once and confirm. Every mail has an unsubscribe link. You can export the list.
  • Views and referrers.Counted from the server log, per article and for the whole site. No cookies, so nothing to consent to.
  • Passwords.One for the whole blog, or one article at a time. Readers type it once.
  • User management, kept small.The accounts come from one environment variable, and that is all of it. You decide who can write when you install it, and change it there.
  • One container, SQLite, photos as files.Back up the folder and the blog is backed up. The public look is one CSS file you edit in Settings.

It leaves out

  • Plugins and a theme store.
  • A sign-up page and user roles to hand out.
  • A comment queue to work through.
  • Analytics scripts and a cookie banner.
  • Anything else to run: no CDN, no mail service, no image host, no captcha.
  • A build step and a laptop. You can write the whole thing on a phone.
  • AI.

If the left list is what you need and you do not miss anything on the right, this will suit you. If you do miss something on the right, get Ghost or WordPress instead.

Run it yourself

Texttile is one Docker container. The database, the original photos and the smaller copies it makes for the web all sit in one folder next to it. Updating means pulling the new image.

docker run -d --name texttile \
  -p 4000:4000 \
  -v texttile-data:/data \
  -e SECRET_KEY_BASE="$(openssl rand -base64 48)" \
  -e PHX_HOST=blog.example.com \
  -e ADMIN_USERS=you,julia \
  ghcr.io/texttile-blog/texttile:latest

Those environment variables are the whole setup: who can write, the address the blog answers on, and one secret. SQLite, the original photos and the copies made for the web all live in /data. Everything you might change later is in Settings, and nothing there has a Save button.
