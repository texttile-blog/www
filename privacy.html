Privacy

This page is www.texttile.blog, the product page for Texttile. It has no account and no cookie. It counts how it is read, and it takes one email address, in the demo form. This is the whole list.

Last changed in August 2026.

Who is responsible

Klaus Breyer runs this page and answers for it. The address and the other details of the law are in the imprint. You reach a person at klaus@texttile.blog.

Where the page runs

GitHub Pages serves the files. Cloudflare sits in front of the domain. Both companies see the request that brings this page to you: your IP address, the time, the page, and what your browser says about itself. They keep that for a short time, to serve the page and to stop attacks. The legal ground is our interest in a page that stays up (Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR).

What the page counts

We count how the page is read, with PostHog on its European servers. PostHog writes nothing to your browser here. It is set to keep its id in the tab, not in a cookie and not in local storage, so the id ends when you close the tab. This is why the page asks you for nothing before it counts.

  • The visit.The page you opened, when, and where you came from.
  • The clicks.Which link or button you used, with its text and its address.
  • The reading.Which sections came onto your screen, how far down you got, and whether you copied the docker line.

PostHog gets your IP address with each of these, and it derives a rough place from it. We see a count, not a person. The legal ground is our interest in a page we can make better (Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR). The whole of the counting is one file you can read: assets/js/analytics.js.

The demo blog

"Try it out" starts a real Texttile for you. Warper, our own service, creates it on Fly in a Frankfurt datacenter, and destroys it one hour later with everything you put in it.

  • Your email address.You type it in the form, and it does two things. It becomes the admin account of your demo blog, which sends one mail to it with the link that sets your password. That copy dies with the demo. The second copy stays with us, and we use it once, to ask you what you think of Texttile. It goes to nobody else. The mail from the demo goes out through Resend, the service that carries our mail. The checkbox next to it is your consent (Art. 6 (1) (a) GDPR), and you can take it back at any time, with one mail.
  • A record of the demo.The name of the machine, its address, the hour it ends, the time of your consent, and your IP address as a hash. The hash is how the service holds one demo per connection at a time.
  • What you write in the demo.It stays in the demo, and it goes with the demo. After the hour, the machine and its disk are destroyed. Nothing is copied out of it and nothing is read by us.

Please treat the demo as a public room. Everybody who has its address can read everything in it, and everybody you give your password to can write. Do not put anything in it that has to stay private.

Mail

If you write to klaus@texttile.blog, we keep your mail as long as the answer needs it, and as long as the tax and trade law asks us to keep letters of business.

What you can ask for

You can ask us what we hold about you, ask us to correct it, and ask us to delete it. You can object to the counting. You can also complain to a data protection authority. One mail to klaus@texttile.blog is enough for the first three.

The product is not this page

Texttile itself is a container you run on your own machine, and it sends us nothing. This page describes what happens here, on www.texttile.blog, and in the demo we run for you.
